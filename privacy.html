Privacy Policy

1. Overview

Channel Crossings ("we", "us", "our") is a website that shows daily figures for people arriving in the UK by small boat across the English Channel, together with current weather conditions in the Channel. This policy explains what information is involved when you visit the site and what choices you have.

The short version: we do not collect or store any personal information about you when you visit. There are no accounts, no advertising and no tracking. The only exceptions are if you sign up for email updates, or choose to email us.

2. Information We Collect

We do not collect personal information when you visit the site. In particular, the site does not use:

  • accounts, logins or contact forms (the email updates sign-up is the only form, see below);
  • analytics or visitor-tracking tools;
  • advertising or advertising networks;
  • tracking cookies of any kind.

Weekly roundup emails. If you sign up for the weekly roundup of the figures, we hold your email address and use it only to send you the roundup. Nothing else is asked for. You sign up by choice: after you enter your address you are sent a message asking you to confirm, and nothing more is sent until you do. Every email has an unsubscribe link, and unsubscribing stops the emails straight away. We keep your address until you unsubscribe or ask us to delete it. We never sell or share it. The mailing list is run for us by EmailOctopus (Three Hearts Digital Ltd, London), which stores the list and sends the emails on our behalf under EmailOctopus's Privacy Policy. Like most email services, it can record whether an email is opened and which links are clicked.

We do not ask for your location. The weather shown on the site is for the Channel itself: the Dover Strait, and any spot on the map you tap or point at. It is never for where you are.

If you email us. If you choose to contact us at x.chancrossings@gmail.com, we receive your email address and whatever you include in your message. We use this only to read and reply to you. We do not add you to any mailing list or share your details with anyone. Emails are kept only as long as needed to deal with your message, and you can ask us to delete them at any time. Our email is provided by Google (Gmail), which processes messages under Google's Privacy Policy.

3. Information Stored on Your Device

To make the site work better, your browser keeps a few things on your own device:

  • Closed notices: if you close the "New figures incoming" notice, your browser remembers that for the rest of the day so it does not keep reappearing.
  • The map tour: that you have seen the quick tour of the map, so it is not shown again.
  • Closed weekly roundup box: if you close the weekly roundup sign-up, so it stays closed.
  • Your appearance choice: if you pick Day or Night instead of Auto.
  • Closed breaking news: the last news story you closed, so it stays closed until a newer one arrives.
  • Your last visit: the date of the latest figures you saw, so the site can mark figures that are new since then.
  • An offline copy: a saved copy of the site's pages and figures, so it opens quickly and still works without a signal.

All of this stays on your device and is never sent to us or anyone else. You can remove it at any time by clearing your browser's data for this site.

4. Third-Party Services

Like every website, Channel Crossings relies on a few outside services to work. When your browser connects to them, they receive the technical details that any web request carries, such as your IP address and browser type. Each handles that information under its own privacy policy:

  • GitHub Pages hosts the website. GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security and to keep the service running. See GitHub's Privacy Statement.
  • Open-Meteo provides the weather forecasts. Your browser requests the forecast directly from Open-Meteo each time the page loads and every 30 minutes while it is open. See Open-Meteo's terms and privacy information.
  • Esri provides the map itself. Your browser downloads the map images from Esri's servers as the map is shown and moved. See Esri's privacy statement.
  • Amazon Web Services hosts the Terrain Tiles, the heights of the land that shade the hills on the map's Temperature view. Your browser downloads them from Amazon's servers only while Temperature is showing. See AWS's Privacy Notice.
  • unpkg delivers Leaflet, the free software that draws the map, each time the page loads. See Cloudflare's privacy policy (unpkg runs on Cloudflare).
  • EmailOctopus runs the email updates list. Your browser only contacts it if you sign up. See EmailOctopus's Privacy Policy.
  • Our domain name provider directs visitors from our web address to the site. It does not see what you do on the site.

The crossing figures themselves are collected from GOV.UK by an automated process on GitHub's servers. Your browser only contacts GOV.UK if you tap Refresh Figures, when it reads the Home Office's public daily figures page directly (see GOV.UK's privacy notice).

Share buttons. The X, Facebook, WhatsApp and Telegram buttons are plain links. Nothing is loaded from those services and nothing is shared with them unless you tap a button, which opens their site or app with a ready-made post under their own privacy policy.

5. Cookies

Channel Crossings does not set any cookies. The items described in Section 3 use your browser's own storage, which is not shared with anyone.

6. Links to Other Websites

The site links to other websites, including GOV.UK and Open-Meteo. We are not responsible for how those sites handle your information. Please read their privacy policies.

7. Children

Channel Crossings is a general-audience information site. Email updates are intended for adults, and we do not knowingly hold the email address of anyone under 13.

8. Your Rights

Under UK data protection law (the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018) you have rights over personal data held about you, including the right to access it and to ask for it to be deleted. Because we do not hold personal data about visitors, there is normally nothing for us to provide or delete, apart from your email address if you have signed up for email updates, and any emails you have sent us (see Section 2), which we will provide or delete on request. For information held by the services in Section 4, contact them directly. If you have concerns, you can also contact the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

9. Changes to This Policy

If the site changes in a way that affects your privacy, for example by adding a contact form or analytics, we will update this policy first and change the date at the top of this page.

2. What the Site Does

The Site is a free information service. It shows the number of people detected arriving in the UK by small boat each day, running totals, and a general indication of weather conditions in the Channel.

3. Where the Figures Come From

Arrival figures are taken from the Home Office's published data, Small boat activity in the English Channel, on GOV.UK. The Home Office describes these figures as provisional. They can be revised, including downwards, and final figures are published later in the quarterly Immigration System Statistics.

Channel Crossings is an independent site. It is not run by, affiliated with or endorsed by the Home Office, Border Force or any part of the UK Government.

Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

Year-to-date totals and comparisons with earlier years are calculated by the Site from the published figures.

5. Channel Conditions

The Channel conditions notice uses forecast data from Open-Meteo, licensed under CC BY 4.0. The "calm", "moderate" and "rough" ratings are a simple guide based on wind speed and wave height at one point in the Dover Strait, used to give context to the arrival figures.

This is not a marine forecast. Do not use it for navigation or to decide whether it is safe to go to sea. Conditions in the Channel change quickly and it is one of the busiest shipping lanes in the world. For sea-safety information, use the Met Office Shipping Forecast and official maritime sources.
